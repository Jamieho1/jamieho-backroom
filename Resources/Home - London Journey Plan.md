## Continuous scroll storytelling — confirmed requirement

On 2026-11-03 Jamie clarified that this applies to EVERY narrative chapter, not only the opening or the connections. The main page must feel like one continuous premium spatial journey, with the environment and information progressing together as the visitor scrolls. A series of static landmark photographs with text fades, simple image zooms or crossfades does not satisfy this brief.

- Within each chapter, scroll progress advances a coherent camera/depth sequence and reveals information in deliberate stages. The chapter is more than a single wide background with an overlay. Layered imagery may be source material, but it must support composed spatial motion rather than a gallery treatment.
- Information changes with the story: concise context, technology, experience milestones, project evidence and Articles/channels appear at meaningful positions. Do not animate every line continuously; provide composed reading holds and keep the semantic text usable. Ambient life can continue while scroll-controlled travel pauses.
- Every chapter storyboard must specify its entry view, camera route, depth layers, staged information, readable holds and exit connection. Connections match perspective, movement, lighting and visual focus so the next location feels part of the same journey. Exact shots are still to be discussed.
- The pilot must prove movement AND staged information inside a chapter, plus one connection and the room/monitor interaction. A polished transition between otherwise static sections is insufficient. Inspect intermediate and reverse states, not just chapter endpoints or an exported movie.
- Use the requested scroll-craft skill to develop the narrative/layer/storyboard and verify rendered scroll states; Three.js, GSAP and parallax coordinate the live stage and real HTML. Revisit the relevant skill instructions when building. Use HyperFrames' seekable/deterministic authoring workflow for motion studies or selected assets where appropriate; it does not replace the interactive site. ## How the experience stays expandable

- One visual module per location, with defined camera/visual entry and exit, its content anchors and neighbouring connection. Adding a location changes those connections rather than requiring regeneration of an entire long film.
- Separate location visuals from editable profile, experience, project and article content. New content should not require a new background render or burned-in text.
- A shared room/monitor transition handles every app. Adding a future app should reuse the same entrance/close behavior and registry/access rules.
- One scroll-progress coordinator drives scene travel, parallax and semantic copy. Ambient motion has a coordinated clock; it does not respond to the mouse. Do not stack competing scroll drivers.
- Chunk/loading boundaries follow locations and direct destinations. The room can load as a destination without fetching/playing every earlier location. Direct links, no-WebGL and no-JavaScript reading/navigation remain functional.
- Public routes keep prerendered meaningful content. Visual enhancement must not replace the whole page with inaccessible baked text or an empty spacer-only document when scripts are unavailable.
- Preserve the existing app forms/editors and drafts; app internals are outside this main-page visual redesign. Their eventual colour/layout work has a separate scope.
