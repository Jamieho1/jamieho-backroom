## Confirmed experience rules

- The scenery fills the viewport. The final phone version must also feel like a scene, with deliberately placed readable overlays and no sideways scrolling at 390px. The prototype's former separate upper text band is superseded.
- Slow camera travel while reading, smoothly faster travel between panels. Scroll owns position, not a timed ride. Stop and reverse anywhere, including inside joins. No forced snap, automatic finish or mandatory tour.
- Stage one useful panel at a time. Side or restrained glass panels enter, accompany the scene briefly and move upward/out as the journey progresses. Keep text face-on enough to read. Personal copy, navigation and actions are real HTML backed by code or approved database content.
- Readable pauses do not require a lifeless scene. Appropriate clouds, water, reflections and distant activity can continue independently. The camera must not continue travelling after scrolling stops.
- No mouse-driven camera/character tilting. No scene-wide wave shader, compressed bodies, bending architecture or drifting shared contact points.
- A location is more than a static photo with text fades. Production needs convincing viewpoint change and coherent motion. Any 2D pan/zoom previews must be labelled as studies rather than final camera travel.
- Keep first, middle and final camera views compatible. Never fade between unrelated far and close photos to simulate walking. A bridge cannot appear suddenly as an unrelated primitive model.

## Scene and content mapping

Imperial introduces a concise background and curated technology groups. The imagined library has a cool interior and warmer natural lawn outside. Its desk is a thin white strip. Exactly one Imperial-crest notebook and an adjacent unbranded red cup are fixed in world space: absent on arrival, encountered midway, then passed and absent on exit.

Queen's Lawn retains the accepted generated angle, natural slightly darker grass, diagonal paved route from bottom-left toward back/top-right, blue IMPERIAL panel on grey stone at left, path among right trees, shallow tower-base steps and left benches. The interior is imagined, not a claim about a real library seat or floor.

Westminster presents the experience journey through approved public milestones and project branches. Palace/Big Ben remain across the water; the green bridge starts to the right and grows closer consistently. A larger glass block can use the sky as the initial reading area. Move toward the bridge, under its actual-looking structure and into a black cover. Foot travel must follow a credible pedestrian route; a water-level reference alone does not establish one.

St Paul's uses the supplied more distant cathedral view between reflective glass buildings. On desktop, place the scene on the right and white text on black on the left. Reveal the view vertically from black as if descending; continue downward until the scene and text give way to a full black frame. Nearby structure/floor edges pass upward faster than the distant cathedral. This is the projects chapter. Do not substitute a huge close-up dome, invented cabin or repeated giant floor slabs.

Canary Wharf is the Elizabeth line station, with purple roundel, cylindrical stainless columns, slatted ceiling, platform screen doors, benches and grey flooring. Move out from a wall to reveal the logo, then forward toward the train side. Articles and social channels belong here. Use the existing Articles source for LinkedIn, YouTube and Instagram URLs; do not create a second conflicting list.

The train uses Elizabeth line materials, purple patterned seats, poles, glazing and doorway relationships consistent with the supplied references. Board from the side, then move right through a carriage-frame cover into the room. Do not expose incompatible station and outdoor carriage scenery together during a join.

The room is dark, calm, warm and biophilic: walnut standing desk/riser, charcoal walls and rug, vertical acoustic wood slats, black mesh ergonomic chair, amber indirect corner lighting, golden disc wall lamp and shelf sphere. Include one large monitor on arm, light bar, keyboard/wrist rest, vertical mouse, desk mat, tablet, left microphone boom, two wood shelves, orange design book, ceramics, small mirror, simple framed art and plants. Arrive slightly angled, then glide straight toward a large usable monitor. Keep it relaxing rather than a rainbow-lit gaming showroom.
